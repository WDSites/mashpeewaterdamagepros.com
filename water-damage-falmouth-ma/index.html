Water Damage Restoration Falmouth, MA Near Mashpee

Falmouth is the larger town next door to Mashpee, with coastal flooding on its shore streets and a lot of older and seasonal houses. The emergency line covers it 24/7.

When Water Is Spreading in Falmouth

If water is moving through a Falmouth house right now, shut the main water valve when you can reach it dry, keep out of any room with wet outlets and ring the emergency number. Crews serve Falmouth from the Mashpee base, and the line is answered around the clock.

Falmouth has two different kinds of water emergencies, and which one you have changes the response. Plumbing and appliance failures are the same here as anywhere. Coastal flooding is the local pattern, and it is treated as contaminated water from the first minute.

Tell the dispatcher which kind it is

Mention if it was a supply line, a dishwasher, a roof leak or the tide. That tells the crew whether to plan for clean water or contaminated water.

Falmouth's Coastal Flooding Record

Falmouth's 2022 Multi-Hazard Mitigation Plan lists the town's flood exposure in plain terms. The effective FEMA flood map has AE, AO and VE zones, and FEMA reported 26 residential repetitive-loss properties in 2017. That means 26 homes had flooded and been paid on more than once.

Two places in the plan matter for Falmouth village specifically. The intersection of Main Street and Falmouth Heights Road floods in minor events, and Surf Drive was closed by coastal flooding in October 2018. A road closure matters to a water loss because it can slow a crew and cut access to a house.

The Woods Hole tide gauge shows relative sea level rising about 2.98 millimeters a year from 1932 to 2021. For a homeowner, the practical point is that coastal streets that flood occasionally today are not likely to flood less over time. Look up your address on the FEMA flood map, and photograph the property while it is in good condition.

Older Houses and Seasonal Homes

The town's housing plan counts 22,817 housing units in the 2020 Census, with about 30 percent of them seasonal. About 62 percent of units were built before 1980, and only about 10 percent in 2000 or later.

Those two numbers shape water damage. An older house is more likely to have old supply lines, original plaster and layered additions. A seasonal house can sit empty while a small leak runs for days. Neither problem is dramatic until someone walks in.

If you own a seasonal Falmouth house, ask a neighbor or caretaker to check inside after storms and cold snaps, and know where your main water shutoff is before you leave. A small drip found early is far cheaper than a wet floor found in June.

Sewer, Septic and Water Supply

A USGS study published in 2025 found that only about 11 percent of developed properties in Falmouth were on municipal sewer in 2023, with septic systems handling most of the rest. Great Pond sewer work is expected to bring the Maravista area on line around 2027.

For a water loss, this matters when the water is sewage. A backup in a septic neighborhood is a different problem from a backup on a municipal line, and your plumber or septic contractor needs to be part of the fix. We handle the cleanup and drying, not the septic repair.

Town water comes from the Falmouth Water Department. The USGS report says Long Pond supplies about 64 percent of municipal supply. If a leak is in the service line between the street and your meter, the department is the right call.

How We Help Falmouth Homes

Falmouth losses usually start with water mitigation to stop the spread, and storm or tide water moves into flood restoration because of the contamination. Once the structure is dry, rebuild work restores finishes, and if moisture lingered, mold work follows.

Falmouth neighbors East Falmouth and Mashpee. If your home is south of Route 28 toward Waquoit Bay, see East Falmouth, and for losses inland on the Mashpee side, see our Mashpee service area. Drying goals and documentation follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration, which any company you hire should be able to explain.

Permits, Landmarks and Local Contacts

The Falmouth Building Department is in Town Hall at 59 Town Hall Square and runs online permitting. Rebuild work after a loss can involve a permit, so ask the department before you start.

Falmouth Hospital, Beebe Woods and Highfield Hall are some of the places that mark the village for residents. For restoration work, the practical map is simpler. Water gets in from the plumbing, the roof and the shore, and each has its own fix.

Before We Arrive in Falmouth

Stop the source

Close the main water valve if the water is from a pipe and you can reach it safely.

Power and safety

Turn off power to wet areas only if the switch or panel is dry. If any electrical or gas risk exists, leave and call 911 or the utility.

Lift and protect

Raise valuables, small furniture and paper goods off wet floors.

Keep clear of dirty water

Keep people and pets out of floodwater and sewage.

Document

Photograph damage and, for floodwater, the line it left on the walls.

Questions From Falmouth Homeowners

Do you cover every ZIP code in Falmouth?

We serve 02540 in Falmouth village and 02543 in Woods Hole. East Falmouth, Teaticket, Waquoit and Hatchville share ZIP 02536, which has its own East Falmouth page. If your street is near a ZIP boundary, call and we will confirm.

Is emergency water removal available in Falmouth?

Yes. A live dispatcher takes calls through the night, on holidays and across weekends, and a Falmouth burst pipe or storm-tide intrusion is treated as an active loss. Same-day response is the goal when crews are free, though no arrival time is promised.

Is the Main Street and Falmouth Heights Road area a flood risk?

The town's hazard plan says that intersection floods in minor events. If your home is near it, pull up your address on the FEMA flood map and ask the town about flood zone status.

My Falmouth house is seasonal. What should I do before I leave?

Know where the main shutoff is, ask someone to check inside after storms and cold weather, and consider having a plumber look at old supply lines. A leak in an empty house runs unseen.
