Sewage Backup Cleanup in Mashpee, MA

Sewage coming back up a drain is a contamination job first and a drying job second. The 24/7 line is open for Mashpee homes with backed-up toilets, floor drains and septic systems.

Why Sewage Comes Back Into a Mashpee Home

A sewage backup means wastewater is moving the wrong way, out of a drain or toilet instead of away from the house. The usual causes are a blocked house line, a full or failing septic tank, or a blockage in a public main. It exits at the lowest opening, so a basement floor drain often shows it first.

Mashpee is adding public sewer in stages. The town's sewer overview says the State approved the wastewater plan in 2015 and that ground was broken on the treatment facility in December 2022. Until your street is connected, your house drains to a private on-site system, so the first thing to learn is which kind you have.

First-Hour Safety Steps When Sewage Is Inside

Your goal in the first hour is to stop adding water and keep people out of the contaminated rooms.

  • Stop using toilets, sinks, showers and the washing machine. Every gallon goes to the same blocked line. Keep children and pets out of the wet rooms.
  • Cut power to the room only if the panel is dry and reachable without wading. Otherwise call the utility, or 911 if you see sparks or smell gas.
  • If you must go in, the CDC advises an N95 or better respirator, goggles, heavy gloves and waterproof boots.
  • Call a plumber for a blocked line or a septic professional for a full tank, then call the 24/7 line for cleanup.

Sewage on the floor? Leave it for the crew

Do not mop it, vacuum it or spray household cleaner on it. Tell the dispatcher where it is, how far it spread and whether the power is off.

Why Sewage Is Treated Differently

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard defines Category 3 water as grossly contaminated and lists sewage among its examples. Porous materials that soaked it up come out of the house instead of being dried in place.

The EPA advises removing and discarding anything wet for more than 24 to 48 hours after flooding, and it recommends a professional with experience when the water was sewage. The CDC adds that sewage-contaminated drywall and insulation should be discarded. Carpet, pad and mattresses follow the same rule.

How a Sewage Cleanup Runs

  1. Suit up and contain. Crews wear protective gear and hang plastic sheeting over doorways so the rest of the house stays clean.
  2. Extract. Submersible pumps and extraction units pull the liquid out, starting at the deepest point.
  3. Remove porous materials. Carpet, pad, insulation and the lower section of wet drywall are cut out and bagged.
  4. Wash and disinfect. Floors, studs and other hard surfaces are scrubbed with detergent and treated with an antimicrobial.
  5. Dry. Air movers and dehumidifiers run while moisture meters track what is still damp inside the walls.
  6. Verify and document. Readings and photos are recorded for you and your insurer.

How drying works after disinfecting is covered under structural drying and dehumidification. When readings come back normal, water damage repair replaces what was cut out.

Septic Systems, Title 5 and the Board of Health

Under Title 5, the state septic rule, system backup is one of the factors that decides whether a system has failed. MassDEP says a failed system must be upgraded within 2 years, or sooner if the Board of Health finds an imminent health hazard.

Mashpee's Board of Health is at 16 Great Neck Road North and its number is (508) 539-1426. Call it when sewage surfaces in the yard or backs up from a tank, because a cleanup crew cannot repair the system itself. Title 5 inspections are also required within 2 years of a sale, so buyers and sellers should ask what the last one found.

What Changes the Cost of a Sewage Cleanup

Price follows what the water reached and how long it sat, and it is set after someone has seen the rooms.

  • How much liquid is present and how many rooms it reached.
  • Which materials soaked it up: carpet, pad, drywall, insulation, subfloor.
  • How many days of drying equipment the structure needs.

Insurance and Sewer Backup

Sewer backup is a common exclusion. The state Division of Insurance notes that basic home, renter and condo policies leave it out, though some insurers sell sewer or drain backup coverage as an add-on. Neglect and wear and tear are not covered either.

Mistakes That Make a Backup Worse

  • Running water or flushing to see whether the line has cleared.
  • Using a household vacuum on sewage.
  • Keeping wet carpet or a mattress because it looks fine after a day.

Common Questions

Is emergency sewage backup cleanup available in Mashpee?

Yes. On a 2 a.m. sewage call the crew suits up in protective gear, seals off the affected rooms with plastic sheeting and starts pumping. Timing depends on the night's calls and roads and cannot be promised, so say how far the sewage spread.

Who do I call first, a plumber or a cleanup crew?

If sewage is still coming up, call a plumber or septic professional to stop it, since cleanup is wasted while the source is active. Call the cleanup line in the same hour.

Where do I report a failing septic system in Mashpee?

Call the Mashpee Board of Health at (508) 539-1426. It is at 16 Great Neck Road North. State rules treat system backup as a failure factor, so the board is the right place to ask about next steps.

Can carpet and drywall be saved after a sewage backup?

Usually not. The CDC and EPA both advise discarding porous materials such as carpet, pad and sewage-soaked drywall. Hard surfaces and framing can be washed and disinfected.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for a sewage backup?

Often not. Standard policies commonly leave out sewer backup unless an endorsement was added, so read your declarations page or ask your agent before filing.
