Storm Water Damage in Mashpee, MA

Storms put water into houses through roofs, windows, walls and basements, often while the power is out. The 24/7 line is open for Mashpee homes hit by wind, rain and rising water.

How Storms Put Water Into Mashpee Homes

The town's 2017 hazard mitigation plan says Mashpee has a strong history of nor'easters and a history of high winds. It also says flooding here comes from coastal storms, nor'easters and heavy rain.

That gives storm water several ways in: wind-driven rain through windows and siding, a roof or flashing damaged by wind, a limb or tree through the envelope, and rising water at the lowest level. Each one needs a different fix, so the first question is where the water entered.

Mashpee Storm History and What It Means for You

Past storms are on record. The plan lists flooding along Shore Drive from Hurricane Carol in 1954, and flooding of the causeway near Amy Brown Road from Hurricane Bob in 1991. Both were shoreline events. Flooded roads slow every crew, so a wet house deserves an early call.

The plan's hurricane table says even a Category 1 storm, with winds of 74 to 95 miles per hour, can snap large branches, topple shallowly rooted trees and cause power outages. Without power a sump pump stops, so water that would have been held back can come up through the slab.

Wind Rules in the Code

The Massachusetts residential code table lists Mashpee with an ultimate design wind speed of 130 miles per hour and marks it as a windborne debris region. That is a design value for building, not a forecast.

For a homeowner it means flying debris can break windows and doors in a storm, and a broken opening lets rain pour in. Older houses may not match today's code, so check windows, doors and roof edges after any strong storm.

First-Hour Safety Steps After a Storm

  • Stay away from downed lines and treat every one as live. Call 911 for lines, fires or injuries.
  • Do not enter a flooded basement to reach the panel or a pump, because standing water can carry current.
  • Mashpee Fire and Rescue is at 20 Frank E. Hicks Drive, and 911 is the emergency number.
  • If a pipe broke, close the main water valve. If you smell gas, leave and call 911.
  • Photograph the damage and the water line before moving anything, and keep a list of what was lost.

Rain Water or Contaminated Water

The IICRC says wind-driven rain is not automatically Category 3, because the water must actually be grossly contaminated. Rain that came through a window and soaked drywall is usually a drying job. Storm surge or water mixed with sewage is different and is treated as contaminated.

That matters for cost and scope. Clean rain water lets the crew dry materials in place, while contaminated water means removal, as described under flood damage restoration and sewage backup cleanup.

Our Storm Water Damage Process

  1. Triage. The crew checks power, structure and where the water entered.
  2. Extract. Submersible pumps clear standing water, and extraction units lift it from carpet.
  3. Open and inspect. Moisture meters read ceilings and walls, and a thermal imaging camera helps trace wet cavities.
  4. Remove what cannot dry. Soaked pad, insulation and any contaminated material is bagged.
  5. Dry. Air movers and dehumidifiers run, with generator power if the grid is down.
  6. Monitor and record. Readings and photos go in the file until materials reach the drying goal.

A broken roof or window needs a temporary cover and a permanent repair from the right trade. We handle the water, and water damage repair follows once the structure is dry.

What Changes the Cost After a Storm

  • Whether the water was rain, surge or sewage-mixed.
  • How many rooms and floors it reached.
  • Which materials soaked: carpet, drywall, insulation, hardwood.
  • Days of drying equipment, and whether power must be supplied.
  • Whether contents need sorting and disposal.

Insurance: Wind, Rain and Flood

Rising water and rain through a broken opening are different questions on a policy. State guidance says flood is a separate coverage, and a new flood policy usually waits 30 days before it starts, so it will not help a storm already under way.

Tell your insurer exactly how the water got in. Ask whether storm damage carries its own deductible, and photograph everything before repairs.

Common Questions

Is emergency storm water damage cleanup available in Mashpee?

Yes. During a storm, calls with water still rising or a live electrical risk go to the front, and the crew starts with pumping. No arrival time is promised, because blocked roads and downed trees change it.

What wind speed does the state code use for Mashpee?

The Massachusetts residential code table lists an ultimate design wind speed of 130 miles per hour for Mashpee and treats the town as a windborne debris region. It is a design value for building and not a forecast.

Is rain that came through a window treated as sewage-level contamination?

Not automatically. The IICRC says wind-driven rain is not Category 3 unless it is grossly contaminated. A technician checks what the water touched before deciding.

Will my homeowners policy cover storm water damage?

It depends on how the water entered. Flood needs a separate policy under state guidance, so ask your insurer separately about wind and rain.

What do I do if the power is out and water is coming in?

Stay out of flooded areas near the panel, close the main water valve if a pipe broke, call 911 for electrical or gas danger and call the 24/7 line. Dehumidifiers need power, so the crew may bring a generator.

Where is Mashpee Fire and Rescue?

It is at 20 Frank E. Hicks Drive, Mashpee, and 911 is the emergency number.
